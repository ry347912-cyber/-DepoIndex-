 1  Q. Looking at Exhibit 1, could you describe your first legal position after law school?
 2  A. After law school, I completed a fellowship at the National Consumer Law Center.

 6  A. Yes, both representing borrowers in bankruptcy proceedings and state debt collection defense.

 1  Q. Ms. Yu, continuing on Page 7, let's detail your employment history from 2012 to 2020.
 2  A. During those years at NCLC, my primary focus was student loan debt relief and servicer oversight.
 3  Q. Did you regularly review loan servicing agreements between lenders and third-party servicers?
 4  A. Yes, I reviewed dozens of master servicing agreements, origination contracts, and collection notes.
 5  Q. What specific elements do you evaluate when reviewing a loan servicing agreement?
 6  A. We evaluate payment hierarchy rules, default triggers, forbearance fees, and servicer incentives.
 7  Q. Why are servicer incentives important in subprime student loan portfolios?
 8  A. Because if a servicer is compensated based on collection volume rather than loan workout success, defaults skyrocket.
 9  Q. In your employment history, have you audited servicer call center scripts?
10  A. Yes, I have reviewed call scripts used by major servicers including Sallie Mae, Navient, and Vervent.
11  Q. Did you find systemic misrepresentations in those call scripts regarding loan terms?
12  A. In many subprime portfolios, yes. Borrowers were steered into costly default status rather than re-enrollment.
13  Q. Did your team publish a landmark report on subprime institutional lending in 2016?
14  A. Yes, entitled 'The Cost of Mismanagement: How Subprime Student Loan Servicing Harms Borrowers.'
15  Q. Did that report analyze the PEAKS loan program specifically?
16  A. Yes, PEAKS Trust 2009-1 was one of the primary case studies featured in the report.
17  Q. What role did Vervent, formerly known as First Associates Loan Servicing, play in that portfolio?
18  A. First Associates was the primary backup servicer and later primary servicer for PEAKS.
19  Q. Did you personally examine First Associates' operational records for that report?
20  A. Yes, including monthly servicing reports, default logs, and borrower dispute files.
21  Q. What was your title at NCLC when that report was published?
22  A. Senior Attorney and Director of the Student Loan Borrower Assistance Project.
23  Q. Were you promoted to Deputy Director of NCLC thereafter?
24  A. Later in 2019, I became Deputy Director before transitioning to the Student Borrower Protection Center.
25  Q. What are your current responsibilities at SBPC?

 1  Q. Ms. Yu, let's turn to your relationship with and investigation of Defendant Vervent, Inc.
 2  A. Very well.
 3  Q. When did you first become aware of Vervent, Inc. in connection with ITT Tech loans?
 4  A. In late 2014, when borrowers reported receiving collection demands from First Associates.
 5  Q. First Associates subsequently changed its name to Vervent, correct?
 6  A. Yes, in 2019 First Associates rebranded as Vervent, Inc.
 7  Q. Did you review the corporate structure and ownership of Vervent?
 8  A. Yes. Vervent is a financial technology and loan servicing company based in San Diego.
 9  Q. What was Vervent's specific role regarding the PEAKS private student loan trust?
10  A. Vervent was hired to perform primary loan servicing, payment processing, and collection enforcement.
11  Q. Did Vervent originate any of the loans in the PEAKS portfolio?
12  A. No. The loans were originated by Access Group and ITT Educational Services, Inc.
13  Q. So Vervent entered as a successor servicer after origination?
14  A. Correct. They assumed active servicing duties when the initial servicer defaulted.
15  Q. How many student loan accounts did Vervent manage under the PEAKS agreement?
16  A. Approximately 45,000 individual student accounts nationwide.
17  Q. What was the aggregate dollar value of the loans serviced by Vervent in PEAKS?
18  A. Roughly $300 million in outstanding principal and accrued interest.
19  Q. Did you have any direct communications with Vervent executives prior to this lawsuit?
20  A. Yes, in 2017 I participated in a roundtable discussion with Vervent's Chief Operating Officer.
21  Q. What was discussed during that roundtable?

 2  A. Very well. I have conducted an extensive review of Vervent's Standard Operating Procedures manual.
 3  Q. What specific SOP documents did you examine?
 4  A. SOP-102 (Payment Processing), SOP-204 (Borrower Deferment), and SOP-309 (Default Escalation).
 5  Q. Let's start with SOP-102. How were partial payments processed under Vervent's procedure?
 6  A. Under SOP-102, if a borrower paid 95% of their monthly installment, Vervent treated the entire account as delinquent.
 7  Q. Did SOP-102 apply the partial payment towards principal and interest first?
 8  A. No. Partial payments were applied first to unpaid late fees, then interest, and zero to principal.
 9  Q. Is that fee-first payment waterfall standard in consumer loan servicing?
10  A. No. Federal student loan servicing standards require applying payments to interest and principal before fees.
11  Q. What happened to a borrower who fell short by $5 on a $300 monthly payment?
12  A. They were charged a $25 late fee, and their partial $295 payment was consumed by fees and interest.
13  Q. So the principal balance never decreased?
14  A. Correct. The borrower entered a negative amortization trap.
15  Q. Now let's look at SOP-204 concerning Borrower Deferment and Forbearance.
16  A. Under SOP-204, call center representatives were trained to offer forbearance before income-driven relief.
17  Q. Why is steering borrowers into forbearance harmful over time?
18  A. Because interest continues to capitalize during forbearance, increasing total loan debt rapidly.
19  Q. Did Vervent's call center scripts require representatives to explain interest capitalization?
20  A. The scripts contained a brief disclaimer, but representatives regularly skipped it during calls.
21  Q. Did you review call recordings of Vervent customer service reps speaking with PEAKS borrowers?
22  A. Yes, I listened to 75 sampled customer call recordings from 2017 to 2019.
23  Q. In what percentage of those calls was interest capitalization properly explained?
24  A. In less than 12% of the sampled calls.
25  Q. Did SOP-309 govern default escalation and automated dialing systems?
